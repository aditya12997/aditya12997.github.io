01 · Selected work

Latest projects.

Open-source builds

Agentic AI · LangGraph

Customer Support Chatbot with LangGraph

A support chatbot built as a graph instead of a single prompt loop. One node classifies each message using structured output and routes it to the right handler, and a separate node independently decides whether the conversation needs to escalate to a human, all tracked through custom state and a checkpointer for multi-turn memory. Includes both node-level and token-level streaming, running entirely on Groq.

Multimodal RAG · CLIP + Vision LLM

Multimodal PDF RAG Pipeline

Indexes both text and embedded images from a PDF into a single CLIP vector space, so one similarity search can retrieve the right chart alongside the right paragraph. Retrieved images are passed straight to a vision-capable Groq model, which can answer questions a text-only pipeline would miss entirely, like reading a number off a chart. Built with PyMuPDF, CLIP, and FAISS, tested against a real BlackRock ETF factsheet.

Code ↗
RAG · LangChain LCEL

RAG Pipeline with LangChain LCEL

A from-scratch retrieval-augmented generation pipeline over PDF documents, chaining load, split, embed, retrieve, and generate into one composable pipeline with LangChain's Expression Language. Runs on local HuggingFace embeddings, Chroma for storage, and Groq for generation, with a working notebook comparing chunk size, retrieval breadth, and temperature side by side.

Code ↗
Retrieval · Image Search

MMR-Diversified Image Search

Plain similarity search on a catalog with near-duplicate photos just returns the same item over and over. This project implements Maximal Marginal Relevance to re-rank results for both relevance and diversity, tested on 15,000+ Amazon product images, with a lambda sweep showing exactly where the trade-off between the two breaks down.

Code ↗
NLP · Sentiment Analysis

Twitter Sentiment Analysis for Uber

Pulled live tweets about Uber through the Twitter API and scored them for polarity and subjectivity to see what riders were actually saying about the service. Results skewed heavily neutral rather than strongly positive or negative, pointing to specific friction points like wait times and payment issues rather than a broad brand problem.

05 · About me

A bit about me.

I didn't start in tech. I started in rooms — client rooms, boardrooms, pitch rooms — learning how to listen well enough to actually solve the right problem. That instinct has never left me, and it's what makes me different from most people who do what I do.

My career sits at a junction most people avoid: commercial enough to walk into a boardroom and shape a strategy, technical enough to go back to my desk and build the system that executes it. Over five years across financial data, credit analytics, and enterprise client delivery, I've learned that the most valuable thing you can bring to any room is the ability to move fluidly between languages — the language of business, the language of data, and the language of the person sitting across the table who just needs their problem solved.

What I've learned is that the gap between a good insight and a real outcome is almost always a people problem, not a technical one. The analysis is rarely the hard part. Getting the right people to trust it, act on it, and build on it — that's where the work actually happens. I've become quite good at that part.

I lead from curiosity. I ask more questions than I give answers, I write things down obsessively, and I tend to spot the problem underneath the problem faster than most. I work best when the stakes are high, the brief is ambiguous, and the team is small enough that everyone's fingerprints are on the outcome.

Based in Madrid. Open to London, Amsterdam, and beyond.
